## Non-clone rule

The current movement-pathway prototype becomes one candidate, not the universal Physiotherapy template. The other 19 candidates must change the visible information rhythm, hero geometry, card system, process visualization, image treatment, conversion presentation, and mobile reflow. A palette, font, image, or business-name swap is not a new candidate.

Dental remains immutable. Physiotherapy candidates must pass both boundaries:

- cross-industry similarity at or below `0.64` against every locked Dental candidate;
- within-Physiotherapy similarity at or below the pack's candidate threshold, with exact opening structures and hero/card/image tuples rejected even when IDs are renamed.

Shared code is limited to accessible primitives, rendering, actions, assets, forms, and release gates. Each candidate owns its visible art direction.

## Candidate identity contract

Every candidate must declare and render:

- hero and navigation composition;
- opening composition and Home section order;
- card system and process visualization;
- surface rhythm and conversion pattern;
- image treatment and candidate-specific motifs;
- mobile reflow, including how overlaps, bands, masks, rails, or diagonals collapse;
- page-specific section order for all six required routes.

The renderer exposes the selected candidate ID as a data attribute so visual CSS and computed-geometry QA can target the actual candidate rather than relying on metadata alone.

## Media expansion

The 12-image reference pool proves one site, not 20 candidates. Before Top-20 visual certification it must grow to at least 30 role-tagged, licensed assets and provide:

- 20 distinct Home hero assignments;
- six distinct page heroes inside every generated site;
- no hero reuse as supporting content;
- role-aware mapping for clinic environment, arrival/access, equipment detail, assessment, guided movement, independent movement, inclusive movement, and verified team media;
- no third-party product branding in commercial candidates;
- no stock image in team or testimonial contexts that could imply clinic staff or patient evidence.
